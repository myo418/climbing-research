Black and white line-art illustration for an instructional diagram. Clean, precise, confident outlines of near-uniform thin weight on a plain white background. No shading, no hatching, no cross-hatching, no stippling, no grey tones, no gradients, no fills, no color. Never fill any shape with solid black: mats, bags, shoes, clothing and equipment are outlines only. No text, no labels, no arrows, no watermark, no frame, no background scenery. Faces are completely blank and carry no expression: draw the outline of the head, the hair and the ears, but NEVER draw eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth or any expression line. Anatomically accurate proportions and realistic form: hands, fingers, limbs and equipment must be correct in shape and proportion. But keep the linework simple: draw only the outlines and the few lines that actually define the form (a joint, an edge, the thickness of a material, a contact point). Do NOT draw skin creases, wrinkles, muscle striations, fabric folds, hair strands or rock surface texture. Rock must read as real rock: an irregular angular mass built from flat facets that meet along hard, slightly broken edges, with a few long cracks and fractures running through the mass, small chipped notches breaking the silhouette, and corners that are never perfectly straight. Never a smooth rounded blob, never a clean rectangular block like sawn timber, never a wall made only of wavy vertical lines. Build the rock from its facet edges and cracks alone — no stippling, no scratching, no hatching, no speckles for texture. A rock wall behind the subject is drawn the same way: a few long facet edges meeting at angles plus one or two cracks, leaving most of the wall blank — not a curtain of long wavy vertical lines. Man-made equipment is the exception and is drawn as accurate mechanical structure: carabiners, bolts, hangers, belay devices, cams, harnesses, shoes and machines keep every part, edge, material thickness, seam, rivet and moving joint in its correct place — drawn with clean straight lines, true circles and even line weight, like a parts diagram. Simplify the human body, never the hardware. Japanese vector technical illustration style, like an instruction manual or a coloring-book outline.
